## Summary
The AI model `BraTS2020_T1_UNet3D` was utilized to perform automated segmentation of brain tumor regions on the provided T1-weighted MRI scans. The analysis focused on identifying three critical tumor components: the Whole Tumor (WT), Tumor Core (TC), and Enhancing Tumor (ET). The segmentation results suggest the presence of abnormal lesions in both the cerebellar region and the cerebral hemisphere, requiring further clinical correlation.

## Clinical Interpretation
The segmentation results indicate a complex tumor profile with evidence of both a central core and peripheral enhancement. The presence of an "Enhancing Tumor" (ET) region often correlates with blood-brain barrier disruption and higher-grade malignancy, such as glioblastoma or high-grade glioma, although this must be confirmed histologically. The spatial distribution across different brain regions suggests either a multifocal primary tumor or metastatic disease. The "Whole Tumor" (WT) boundary helps in estimating the peritumoral edema and the overall mass effect on surrounding neural structures [IMG:000_sample_01_t1_WT.png].

## Recommendations or Limitations
The provided analysis is based solely on T1-weighted imagery; however, a definitive diagnosis of brain tumors typically requires a multi-parametric MRI approach, including T2-weighted and FLAIR sequences to better assess edema and infiltration. These AI-generated masks should be used as a supportive tool for surgical planning or radiotherapy targeting and not as a standalone diagnostic report. It is strongly recommended to correlate these findings with the patient's clinical symptoms and perform a biopsy for histopathological confirmation to determine the exact tumor grade and molecular subtype.
