## Clinical Interpretation
The AI has effectively localized the bilateral SI joints, which is a critical first step in the screening and diagnosis of axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA) or ankylosing spondylitis. By isolating these ROIs, the system facilitates a more focused clinical review for signs of sacroiliitis, such as bone marrow edema, erosions, or joint space narrowing. The high confidence scores (0.94 and 0.93) suggest that the anatomical landmarks were clearly identifiable in this specific slice.

## Recommendations or Limitations
While the detection of the SI joint locations is accurate, this result is limited to anatomical localization and does not provide a diagnostic assessment of inflammatory activity or structural damage. A qualified radiologist or rheumatologist must evaluate the identified ROIs to assess for pathological changes. It is recommended to correlate these findings with clinical symptoms (e.g., inflammatory back pain) and other MRI sequences (such as STIR or T1-weighted images) for a definitive diagnosis.
