## Clinical Interpretation
The high probability values (Left: 93.14%, Right: 89.33%) suggest the presence of bilateral Bone Marrow Edema, which is a hallmark imaging finding for active sacroiliitis. In the context of axial Spondyloarthritis (axSpA) or Ankylosing Spondylitis, these findings often correlate with clinical symptoms such as inflammatory back pain. The Grad-CAM visualization confirms that the model is focusing on the subchondral bone regions of the SI joints, which is clinically consistent with the typical distribution of BME in inflammatory arthritis.

## Recommendations or Limitations
While the AI shows high confidence in detecting BME, these results should be interpreted as supportive evidence rather than a definitive diagnosis. It is recommended that a radiologist or rheumatologist review the original MRI sequences (e.g., STIR or T2-weighted fat-saturated images) to confirm the findings and exclude other potential causes of edema, such as mechanical stress or infection. Clinical correlation with the patient's physical examination and laboratory markers (e.g., CRP, HLA-B27) is essential for a final diagnosis and treatment plan.
