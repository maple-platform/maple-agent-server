## Clinical Interpretation

The AI model identifies bilateral Bone Marrow Edema (BME) with high confidence (Left: 93.14%, Right: 89.33%). In the clinical context of rheumatology, bilateral BME in the SI joints is a significant imaging hallmark of active inflammation, often associated with axial Spondyloarthritis (axSpA) or Ankylosing Spondylitis.

The Grad-CAM overlays provide spatial localization of the suspected edema, allowing the clinician to correlate the AI's focus with the actual hyperintense signals typically seen on STIR or T2-weighted fat-saturated sequences. The fact that both joints show high probabilities suggests a systemic inflammatory process rather than a localized mechanical issue.

## Recommendations or Limitations

While the AI shows high probability scores for BME, these results should not be considered a definitive diagnosis. BME can also occur due to other causes, such as mechanical stress, trauma, or infection, which the AI may not be able to differentiate.

It is recommended that a qualified radiologist or rheumatologist review the original DICOM images to confirm the findings. Clinical correlation with the patient's symptoms (e.g., inflammatory back pain), physical examination, and laboratory markers (e.g., CRP, HLA-B27) is essential for an accurate final diagnosis and treatment plan.
