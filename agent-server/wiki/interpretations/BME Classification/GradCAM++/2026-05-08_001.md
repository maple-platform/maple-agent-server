## Clinical Interpretation

The AI indicates a high likelihood of Bone Marrow Edema in both SI joints, with probabilities exceeding 89%. In a clinical context, bilateral BME is a hallmark finding often associated with active sacroiliitis, which is a key diagnostic criterion for axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA). The Grad-CAM visualization confirms that the model is focusing on the subchondral regions of the SI joints, which is consistent with the typical anatomical distribution of inflammatory edema in these patients.

However, it is important to note that BME can also be observed in other conditions, such as mechanical stress, trauma, or certain infections. Therefore, these AI-generated probabilities should be interpreted as indicators of inflammation rather than a definitive diagnosis of a specific disease.

## Recommendations or Limitations

The results provided by the AI are intended to support clinical decision-making and should not be used as the sole basis for diagnosis. We recommend correlating these findings with the patient's clinical symptoms (e.g., inflammatory back pain), physical examination results, and laboratory markers such as CRP or HLA-B27 status. 

Further review of the raw MRI sequences by a board-certified radiologist is essential to differentiate between inflammatory BME and other potential causes. If the clinical suspicion remains high, longitudinal follow-up imaging may be considered to monitor the progression or resolution of the edema.
