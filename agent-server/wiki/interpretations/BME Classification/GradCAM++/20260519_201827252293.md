## Clinical Interpretation
The high probability scores (Left: 93.14%, Right: 89.33%) suggest the presence of bilateral Bone Marrow Edema (BME). In the clinical context of rheumatology, bilateral BME in the SI joints is a significant imaging hallmark often associated with active inflammation in patients suspected of having axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA) or ankylosing spondylitis.

The Grad-CAM overlay provides spatial localization of the AI's decision-making process, showing concentrated activation areas around the joint margins. This alignment between the AI's "attention" and the anatomical site of inflammation supports the reliability of the classification, though it must be correlated with the patient's clinical symptoms, such as inflammatory back pain.

## Recommendations or Limitations
While the AI shows high confidence in predicting BME, these results should not be used as a definitive diagnosis. AI models can be sensitive to imaging artifacts or other pathological changes that may mimic edema; therefore, a final interpretation by a board-certified radiologist or rheumatologist is mandatory.

It is recommended to correlate these findings with laboratory markers (e.g., CRP, ESR, HLA-B27) and the patient's clinical history. If clinical suspicion remains high despite ambiguous imaging, follow-up MRI or integration with other imaging modalities may be considered to monitor the progression of the inflammation.
